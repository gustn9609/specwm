Abstract

World models come in two flavors that trade quality for speed. Bidirectional world models attend over an entire window of frames and produce temporally coherent, physically consistent rollouts, but every new frame requires a full pass over the window. Autoregressive world models stream frames causally with a KV cache and are fast, but errors compound over long horizons. We present SpecWM, which brings speculative decoding to world models: a small autoregressive world model acts as the draft model and proposes K future frames, and a large bidirectional world model acts as the target model and verifies all K proposals in a single parallel forward pass. Frames are accepted with a rejection-sampling rule that provably preserves the target model's distribution; at the first rejection, the target model's own sample is committed and drafting resumes from there. Because the bidirectional model reads all draft frames jointly, verification costs about one target step regardless of K, so several frames are committed per expensive pass. SpecWM requires no retraining of either model and reaches the rollout quality of the bidirectional world model at a wall-clock close to the autoregressive one.

Method

Speculative decoding needs a cheap proposer and an expensive verifier that can score many proposals in one shot. World models already provide both: the causal model is the natural proposer, and the bidirectional model, which attends over a whole window anyway, is the natural verifier.

① Draft with the autoregressive world model

Given the past frames and the next K actions, the causal model produces x̂t+1, …, x̂t+K step by step with its KV cache. Each step is a small fraction of a target pass, so drafting K frames is still cheap. The draft model only needs to be roughly right; its mistakes are caught in the next stage.

② Verify with the bidirectional world model

The target model sees the context and all K draft frames together. Because it is bidirectional, one forward pass yields the target likelihood of every draft frame conditioned on both past and future, which is exactly what is needed to decide acceptance. The cost of this pass is essentially independent of K.

③ Accept, resample, repeat

Frame i is accepted with probability min(1, ptarget/pdraft), scanning left to right. At the first rejection the target model's corrected frame is committed and later drafts are dropped. The committed frames are appended to the context, and the loop restarts. The output distribution matches sampling from the target model alone.

Why world models are a natural fit

Verification is native

In language models, speculative decoding needs the target to run on the draft tokens in parallel, which a causal LM can do with one extra pass. A bidirectional world model does this by construction: attending over a window of frames is its normal forward pass, so no architectural tricks are needed to score K frames jointly.

Actions make drafts predictable

World models are conditioned on the action sequence. Most of the next frame is determined by the current frame and the action, so a small causal model already proposes plausible frames and the acceptance rate stays high, which is what turns K drafts into several committed frames per target pass.

Error accumulation is bounded

Pure autoregressive rollouts drift because every frame conditions on its own past mistakes. In SpecWM every committed frame has been accepted by, or sampled from, the bidirectional model, so the trajectory never leaves the target model's distribution no matter how long the rollout runs.
