# Action item 2: Speculative decoding (1.3B draft → 14B target) — go/no-go: NO-GO

**Headline:** No-go, on three measured grounds. (1) **Latency:** with the same stack and the same per-frame measurement (Chenfeng method; jar + sandcastle × 3 seeds, one GPU, streamed decode), SDVG with the ImageReward verifier is slower than target-only on average (6 clips, 2 hard prompts) at both thresholds; τ −0.7 is slower on 6/6 clips, τ −2.2 is faster (in fps) on only 1/6 clips per stack. Optimized stack, τ −0.7 / −2.2 vs target-only: gen 51.2 / 43.3 vs 37.8 s per clip (streamed decode on the same GPU included), action→frame p50 4463 / 4156 vs 3237 ms (section 5, f13_latency). (2) **Physics:** on the StEvo-Bench subset (38 tasks, seed 1234, local Qwen3-VL-32B judge, 3-vote majority; not comparable to the paper's Gemini leaderboard; binomial s.e. ≈ 0.05–0.08), SDVG τ −0.7 shows a physical inaccuracy on 0.71 of tasks vs 0.68 for the teacher (27 vs 26 of 38, within noise). The ImageReward verifier brings no physics benefit (section 6). (3) **Bench-43** (86 clips, under load): the paper τ −0.7 is slower than the teacher at equal quality (44.6 vs 35.8 s per clip, decode excluded). τ −2.2 is faster only by becoming student-like (VLM drift 70.6 vs 51.0; section 4). The physics-verifier variant (10/6, original stack) was never faster than target-only (generation 66–128 s vs 62.5–64.4 s per clip). The physics of its final videos (the 10/5 go criterion) was not measured, because its outputs were deleted on 10/9. These setups use different stacks and prompt sets, so their numbers are not compared with each other.

## The question (Guandao, 10/5)
- The test: replace ImageReward with a physics-consistency verifier, then check whether the final videos beat the baselines on physics. ImageReward cannot fix physics by construction.
- Our answer is no-go on cost and on proxy evidence. The test as posed (physics of the final videos) was not completed: see "Not measured" in section 1. Five reasons:
  1. With the physics verifier, SDVG is never faster than target-only (generation 66–128 s vs 62.5–64.4 s per clip, original stack). Draft plus verify cost as much as or more than the 14B work they skip.
  2. Neither off-the-shelf physics scorer (Qwen2.5-VL-7B zero-shot, VideoPhy-2 autorater) separates arms. The StEvo judge separates the draft from the 14B but not the 14B arms from each other.
  3. The 1.3B draft has almost no physics to accept: 1.2 % plausible on StEvo (85 tasks), against 33–48 % for the 14B arms. On the StEvo-38 subset the student shows a physical inaccuracy on all 38 tasks (teacher: 26 of 38).
  4. Even with the cheap ImageReward verifier, SDVG is slower than target-only per frame on average at both thresholds (section 5; 6 clips, τ −2.2 faster on only 1/6 per stack). On bench-43 it is never both fast and teacher-like (section 4). Its accept-all ceiling is ≈ 7.6 fps at student quality.
  5. The ImageReward verifier brings no physics benefit: StEvo-38 physical inaccuracy 0.71 for SDVG τ −0.7 vs 0.68 for the teacher (section 6; one seed, local judge, within noise).
- Pipeline sketch: f01_pipelines. SDVG cost and acceptance: f07_sdvg_analysis. Per-frame latency: f13_latency.

## 1. Physics verifier inside SDVG (10/6; run log only, the videos are gone)
- **Not measured (the go criterion itself).** The output folder (videos, loop.json, lat.pt; `lingbot-world-v2/specwm_out/loop1005fix`) was deleted on 10/9 for disk space, and only the run log remains. There are no physics, drift or quality scores for the resulting videos: no human labels, no StEvo or VLM scores. What was measured: acceptance and per-component time. **Not done:** re-running phys:5 on jar/castle × 3 seeds with the videos kept and scoring them with the StEvo judge. We can offer this as the follow-up if the test as posed is wanted.
- **Setup.** SDVG loop with the 1.3B draft (4 steps) and the 14B target (4 steps). Chunk 0 is always generated by the target, and rejected chunks are regenerated by the target. Both KV caches are updated.
- **Verifier.** Qwen2.5-VL-7B (`lingbot-world-v2/specwm/phys_judge.py`) watches the previous chunk plus the candidate chunk, decoded together, and answers 5 yes/no violation questions (WorldModelBench-style). A chunk is accepted if 5 − #violations ≥ τ.
- **Runs.** jar + castle × seeds 1234/4321/9999, 13 chunks. Source: `exp/queue/phys_verify2_run.log`.
- **Hardware.** The judge needed its own GPU, because the single-GPU attempt ran out of memory (14B + 1.3B + VAE + 7B judge > 95 GB).
- **Stack.** The original stack (run without `--fast`). Draft 8–9 s and commit 15 s per clip match the 10/5 original-stack breakdown (8.5 s and 15.0 s).

This path decodes two chunks per check where the ImageReward path decodes one. The judge-only time was not logged separately.
- **τ=4 timings:** verify runs on all 12 non-forced chunks whatever τ is (draft time is 8–9 s in all 12 runs), so the number of judge calls and the decode length are the same at τ=4 and τ=5. The 2–3× difference (72–100 s vs 32–37 s) therefore points to a measurement confound, such as contention on the judge's GPU, rather than a real cost. It was not diagnosed, so we do not compare the τ=4 verify time against the 47.3 s the 14B spent regenerating.
- **Speed (like for like, original stack, generation only):** no clip is faster than target-only. Physics-SDVG generation took 66–128 s per clip against 62.5–64.4 s for target-only. At τ=5: castle 66–78 vs 62.5 s, jar 90–100 vs 64.4 s.
- **Versus ImageReward SDVG (same scenes, seeds and stack):** the physics verifier is not always slower. On castle at τ=5 its generation (66/73/78 s) is the same as or faster than ImageReward SDVG (78.0/78.0/78.2 s), because it accepts 7–10 draft chunks. It is slower on jar at τ=5 (90–100 vs 80.0–85.7 s) and at τ=4 (102–128 s, with the suspect verify times).
- **Acceptance at τ=4:** the judge passes 11–12 of 13 chunks, so the clip is almost entirely the 1.3B draft. That is consistent with the scorers saturating (section 2), not evidence that the draft's physics is fine.

## 3. Why acceptance stays low: the draft–target gap
- **Token gap (10/6 measurement, facts §7).** Draft chunks are 0.63 σ from the 14B output, and only 7 % of tokens fall within ε. The 14B's own step-1 estimate is closer: 0.35 σ, with 36 % within ε. One 14B step is a better proposal than the whole 4-step 1.3B draft.
- **Noise calibration (README §E, 6 scenes, ε 0.24; a separate measurement).** Mean gap / share within ε: another 14B sample of the same chunk with different noise 0.405 / 34 %, the 1.3B draft 0.607 / 7 %, the 14B's own step-1 estimate 0.308 / 44 %. So the draft is far outside the 14B's own sample-to-sample variability.
- **Finishing from the draft does not help.** Draft-then-Finish runs the 14B for one forward from σ 0.27–0.63 on top of the draft. Quality 5.9–6.2 (draft alone 6.3, teacher 7.6; 12 prompts, seed 1234): the 1.3B has already fixed the layout.
- **Rejecting and redrawing does not help either.** We tried verify + redraw with the 14B drift head (AUROC 0.88 on unseen scenes). Redrawn chunks were flagged again, there was no gain, and time doubled.

## Decision and what replaces it
- **Close cross-model speculative decoding (1.3B draft → 14B target) for this project.** The 14B stays the only generator, and we speed it up directly in three modes: teacher-parity (chunk-wise adaptive 0.7 + safe commit), quality (832, 2-step fused + partial commit) and realtime (704, 2-step fused + partial commit) (items 1 and 3, f05_pareto_bench43, f11_modes).
- **Quiet steady speed of the replacements** (jar + sandcastle, generation only, no decode; facts §15): teacher 4.08 fps; teacher-parity mode 6.80 fps (1.67×), quality mode 9.74 fps (2.39×), realtime mode 15.73 fps (3.85×). Teacher and teacher-parity = chunk-to-chunk period (diff(chunk_start)[5:], n = 6 clips). Ours quality / realtime = cloud-only s/chunk (cloud_chunk_s[6:]; device/decode assumed elsewhere, although in this run the decode was streamed on the same GPU; n = 5, castle s1234 excluded); on one GPU with the decode streamed, ours realtime / quality output 10.8 / 6.9 fps whole-clip (section 5). The bench-43 under-load ratios (1.74× / 2.5× / 4.1× listed; unrounded 1.72× / 2.48× / 3.99×, facts §12) agree within ~4 % (unrounded).
- **What would reopen it:**
  - A draft whose tokens land within the 14B's own noise variability (the 34 % within-ε level, README §E).
  - A physics scorer validated against human labels that separates arms.
  - A verifier much cheaper than decode + reward, combined with high acceptance at teacher quality. Bench-43 shows high acceptance only at student quality.

### Caveats
- **Physics-verifier run (10/6):**
  - What was not measured (the go criterion itself) is stated in section 1. The re-run with videos kept is not done.
  - Scale: 2 scenes × 3 seeds, one judge model, one prompt.
  - The judge ran on a separate GPU.
  - Why verify takes 72–100 s at τ=4 vs 32–37 s at τ=5 was not diagnosed. The judge-call count and decode length are the same at both τ, so a measurement confound (e.g. contention on the judge's GPU) is the likely cause.
- **Saturating scorers:** this is a qualitative finding. No per-arm numbers are in the canonical facts, so do not show a chart of it.
- **StEvo (85 tasks, README §G):**
  - One seed and 85 tasks. The four 10/7-run 14B arms span 38.8–48.2 (9.4 points), 32.9–48.2 (15.3 points) with the Sept 14B run; ~10-point differences are within noise (README §G).
  - The 1.3B row and the 14B official 4-step row come from the September run. The other four 14B arms come from the later run.
  - It uses a local Qwen3-VL-32B judge and a modified protocol, so it is not comparable to the paper's (Gemini-judged) leaderboard.
- **StEvo-38 (section 6):**
  - One seed, 38 tasks: binomial s.e. ≈ 0.05–0.08, so differences < ~0.15 are not significant. The 0.71 vs 0.68 result is "no benefit", not "worse".
  - State evolution and task success are at the floor for every arm.
  - SDVG τ −2.2 (the arm with many draft chunks) and the teacher-parity mode were not run.
  - The task counts, the paired split and SDVG's acceptance on this run were computed 10/12 from the raw per-task reports and loop.json; they are not listed in facts.md.
  - Different task set, frame count and metric from the 85-task run in section 2.
- **Bench-43 fps:**
  - Measured under load. The teacher is 3.9 fps here vs 4.08 fps quiet on the same steady definition but a different prompt set (jar + sandcastle, facts §15); the gap mixes load and prompts. The often-quoted "4.9 fps quiet" is a whole-clip average (gen 31.2 s / 13 chunks), so do not use it as the reference.
  - The quiet timing pass (facts §15) did not include SDVG. The SDVG-vs-target ordering is confirmed separately by the Chenfeng profile (section 5: same stack, no other generation jobs).
  - SDVG's listed 3.5 / 6.4 fps are whole-clip means; on the teacher's steady basis they are 3.1 / 6.7 fps.
- **Chenfeng profile (section 5):**
  - 2 hard prompts × 3 seeds. SDVG accepts fewer draft chunks here than on bench-43 at τ −2.2 (mean 5.2 / 3.8 vs 10.1 of 13), so this profile is less favourable to SDVG at τ −2.2 than bench-43 (where it accepts 10.1 of 13, at student-like quality). At τ −0.7 acceptance is the same as on bench-43 (≈ 1 chunk).
  - Decode is streamed on the same GPU and competes with generation. The two stacks ran at the same time on two GPUs of one host; a VLM judge server ran on another GPU.
  - Whole-clip output fps reads +5 % to +33 % above steady fps; compare steady with steady.
  - Gen s here includes the streamed decode, so it is not comparable with the bench-43 gen s (decode excluded).
  - Ours rows: from a separate, later run (the timing pass, same protocol); n = 5 clips (castle s1234 excluded, cold compile) vs 6 for the other rows. Teacher-parity mode has no row (no streaming decode in spec_early).
- **Prompt sets and stacks differ:** the 10/5 SDVG 99.9 s end-to-end (generation 80.0 s; original stack, jar/castle) and the bench-43 SDVG 44.6 s generation (optimized stack, 43 prompts) are not a speed-up ratio. The same holds for the physics-verifier run (original stack) against any bench-43 number.
- **VLM metrics:** the drift prompt is validated (AUROC 0.82 against human severe-drift labels). The quality prompt is not validated against humans.
- **Accept-all ceiling:** derived from per-chunk costs, never run as its own arm.
- **Draft–target token gap:** measured with a shared-noise latent criterion. It is a statement about paired verification, not about output distributions.

### 발표 메모 (Korean)
- 결론부터: speculative decoding(1.3B draft → 14B target)은 no-go. 이제 근거가 세 가지 측정으로 정리됨: (1) 같은 stack·같은 측정의 per-frame latency, (2) StEvo-38 physics, (3) bench-43 비용/품질. 단, 10/5 테스트("physics verifier로 바꾼 SDVG의 최종 영상이 physics에서 baseline을 이기는가") 자체는 결과 영상이 10/9에 지워져서 측정 못 했다고 먼저 솔직히 말할 것. 재실행(phys:5, jar/castle × 3 seeds, 영상 보존 + StEvo judge 채점)은 안 했고, 원하면 follow-up으로 제안.
- Chenfeng 방식 latency(jar + castle × 3 seed, GPU 한 장, decode도 같은 GPU에서 streaming): SDVG는 두 τ 모두 평균적으로(6개 클립, 어려운 prompt 2개) target-only보다 느림. optimized 기준 클립당 51.2 / 43.3 s vs 37.8 s(같은 GPU의 streamed decode 포함), action→frame p50 4463 / 4156 ms vs 3237 ms, steady fps 2.71 / 3.53 vs 3.80. τ −0.7은 6개 클립 모두 느림. τ −2.2가 이긴 건 stack마다 6개 중 1개뿐이고, draft chunk를 7–10개 받은 경우뿐임. 첫 프레임은 chunk 0을 항상 target이 만들어서 target-only와 거의 같음(1794 vs 1714 ms).
- StEvo-38(로컬 Qwen3-VL-32B judge, seed 1개, 38 tasks — 논문 leaderboard와 비교 불가): SDVG τ −0.7의 physical inaccuracy 0.71 vs teacher 0.68(38개 중 27 vs 26, task별로 SDVG만 4개 / teacher만 3개) → ImageReward verifier는 physics에 이득 없음. "더 나쁘다"가 아니라 "이득 없다"로 말할 것. SDVG 출력은 대부분 target chunk(21개 중 평균 2.8개만 draft). student(draft 모델 단독 실행)는 38개 전부 physical inaccuracy로 판정됨 — SDVG 안에서는 draft가 target history에 conditioning되므로 실제 후보 pool을 측정한 것은 아님.
- Physics judge(Qwen2.5-VL-7B)를 SDVG에 넣었더니 verify 단계(이전+후보 chunk VAE decode + judge)가 τ=5에서 클립당 32–37 s — ImageReward 경로(5.3+3.8 s)보다 훨씬 비쌈. Generation 66–128 s로, 같은 original stack의 target-only generation(62.5–64.4 s)보다 빠른 클립이 하나도 없음. (τ=4의 72–100 s는 judge 호출 수가 같은데 2–3배라 측정 혼선 의심, 진단 안 됨.) castle τ=5는 ImageReward SDVG와 같거나 약간 빠름 — "항상 더 느리다"고 말하지 말 것.
- 근본 원인: draft의 StEvo physics plausibility 1.2 % vs 14B 33–48 %, 토큰도 7 %만 ε 이내 → 제대로 된 verifier라면 거의 다 reject → 속도 이득이 구조적으로 불가능.
- Bench-43(86 클립, optimized stack, under load)에서 ImageReward SDVG: τ −0.7은 teacher보다 느림(클립당 44.6 vs 35.8 s, decode 제외; 같은 steady 기준 3.1 vs 3.9 fps). τ −2.2는 6.4 fps(steady 6.7)지만 drift 70.6으로 student 쪽. Accept-all 상한도 ≈7.6 fps.
- 그래서 14B 하나를 직접 가속하는 쪽으로 감(세 가지 mode: teacher-parity / quality 832 / realtime 704): teacher-parity는 bench-43에서 6.8 fps로 SDVG τ −0.7(3.1 fps, 같은 steady 기준)보다 빠르고 품질은 teacher 수준(Δ −0.07 ± 0.06). ours 704는 drift 56.9로 SDVG τ −2.2보다 fps·drift·quality 모두 우위. 단, ours 704/832의 15.8 / 9.8 fps는 cloud-only(device step은 별도 기기에서 병렬 가정); device step을 같은 GPU에서 직렬로 세면 13.6 / 8.9 fps — 그래도 SDVG τ −2.2(6.4 / 6.7)와 accept-all 상한(7.6)보다 빠름. quiet steady 기준 속도(facts §15): teacher 4.08 fps 대비 teacher-parity 1.67×, quality 2.39×, realtime 3.85×(15.73 fps; ours 832/704는 cloud-only 시간 기준, n = 5). teacher의 "4.9 fps quiet"는 whole-clip 평균이라 기준으로 쓰지 말 것.
