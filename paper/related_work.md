## Autoregressive and streaming video generation

Autoregressive (AR) video models generate frames or latent chunks causally so that a rollout can be extended indefinitely and conditioned on a stream of actions. Early interactive models used discrete tokens (VideoPoet [verify], Genie \cite{genie}), while recent systems keep continuous latents and replace the teacher-forced training of full-sequence diffusion with chunk-wise causal attention and a KV cache: Diffusion Forcing \cite{diffusionforcing} trains with per-token noise levels so that a causal model can denoise a new chunk while attending to clean history; CausVid \cite{causvid} and Self-Forcing \cite{selfforcing} distil a bidirectional teacher into a block-causal few-step student and train on the student's own rollouts to curb exposure bias; MAGI-1 [verify] and Streaming/“live” variants of Wan [verify] scale the same recipe. The Fast model in this work (LingBot-World v2, 14B causal, 4 sampling nodes, sink + sliding-window KV cache) belongs to this family: its speed comes from few-step block-causal denoising, and its failure mode is the one this family shares — small per-chunk errors accumulate along the rollout and the clip drifts away from what the bidirectional teacher would produce. Our measurements make this concrete: with the full clip's history available (a “full-cache oracle”) the drift is unchanged, so the loss is not one of memory but of per-step accuracy.

## Bidirectional video diffusion and few-step distillation

Bidirectional latent diffusion transformers (Wan 2.1/2.2 \cite{wan}, HunyuanVideo [verify], CogVideoX [verify], Movie Gen [verify]) denoise the whole clip jointly with full attention and dozens of sampling steps; Wan 2.2's mixture of a high-noise and a low-noise expert is the Base model here. They are the quality reference but too slow for interaction (70 steps, ~10 min per 10 s clip at 480p in our setting). Step-reduction methods — consistency and latent-consistency models [verify], distribution-matching distillation (DMD/DMD2 \cite{dmd}), adversarial post-training, and the public 4-step LoRAs for Wan 2.2 (Lightning/lightx2v [verify]) — trade a small quality loss for 10–20× fewer steps. Two observations from our experiments connect to this line. First, regression (x0-MSE) objectives are only safe at high noise: every attempt to fine-tune the causal student's low-noise steps with a regression loss produced blurred or frozen video, in line with the argument in DMD that a few-step generator must commit to details through distribution matching rather than through the conditional mean. Second, a bidirectional model run at low resolution and few steps is a surprisingly good *planner*: at 576×304 and 16 steps the Base model follows the camera action and the scene evolution of its 70-step self, at roughly half the cost of a single chunk-wise correction pass.

## World models with action conditioning

Action-conditioned world models (GameNGen [verify], DIAMOND [verify], Oasis [verify], Genie 2/3 [verify], Cosmos [verify], LingBot-World \cite{lingbot}) turn a video generator into a simulator that answers to a control stream. The practical tension is the same one that motivates this work: interactive use demands a causal, few-step model, while fidelity and long-horizon consistency favour a large bidirectional one. Hierarchical and coarse-to-fine designs address the horizon problem: FramePack [verify] compresses distant context, and MilliVid \cite{millivid} learns a hierarchy of temporal resolutions and rolls out coarse-to-fine, reporting that a learned coarse level beats naive downsampling and separating consistency from per-frame quality in evaluation. Our plan → render design is a two-model, two-resolution instance of this idea: the bidirectional model produces a whole-clip plan at low resolution, and the causal model renders it at full resolution one chunk at a time, so the planner provides the global structure that a causal model cannot recover on its own.

## Speculative decoding and draft–verify generation

Speculative decoding \cite{leviathan,chen} accelerates a large language model by letting a small draft propose tokens that the large model verifies in parallel; Medusa and EAGLE [verify] refine the draft with extra heads or feature-level prediction. Transferring the idea to video is not direct, and our early experiments explain why: tokens are discrete and a rejected draft can be replaced exactly, whereas a replaced video chunk — whether the pixels or the draft's KV cache are overwritten — produces a visible seam, and softening the replacement (blending the verifier's prediction into the draft's sampling nodes, re-noising, or hand-off between models) either freezes motion or leaves the seam. Speculative and parallel sampling for diffusion models [verify: e.g. speculative diffusion / parallel Picard-style samplers] work in the same continuous setting but target step parallelism rather than model switching. What survives from the speculative-decoding template in our method is the division of labour — a cheap model proposes, an expensive model provides the information the cheap model lacks — with the information transferred once, at low resolution and before rendering, rather than as a per-chunk accept/reject.

## Cross-model cache and feature transfer

The second axis is how a large model's internal state can be made useful to a small one. In language models, feature-level drafting (EAGLE [verify]) and shared or compressed KV caches (cross-layer sharing, YOCO, cache compression [verify]) show that attention states are transferable across depth and, with a learned map, across models; a linear map between a large and a small model's caches was used in [professor's ICLR submission, verify]. Our ridge probes between the Base and Fast models confirm the structure: the two Wan-derived transformers share the architecture, keys are nearly linearly related in early blocks (R² 0.999 at block 0) and degrade with depth (R² 0.3–0.5 by block 15). We therefore train a per-block linear translator from the planner's low-resolution keys and values into the causal model's cache, positioned with the causal model's rotary embeddings on its own grid, and find that it carries layout and motion as well as an input-channel hint does, with the same requirement we found for the input-channel route — the transferred state must be visible only to the adapted sampling nodes. Injecting low-frequency information and keeping the small model's own high-frequency synthesis is also the principle behind latent-upsampling and cascaded video generation [verify], which our render stage resembles with a causal model in place of a super-resolution cascade.
